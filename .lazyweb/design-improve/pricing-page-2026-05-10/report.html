Lazyweb Design Improve

AR Air pricing should feel like a guided price finder, not a spreadsheet.

The current page is honest and on-brand. The redesign opportunity is to help a stressed HVAC customer find a likely repair price fast, understand what can change the quote, and call or text without hunting.

TL;DR

Keep every transparent price, but restructure the page around search, category chips, common repairs, a sticky quote panel, and a short pricing FAQ.

Current State

Improvement Ideas

1. Add A Price Finder At The Top

Start with a search input, category chips, and common repair cards. This answers the visitor’s first question before they scroll through nearly fifty rows.

┌────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ Search repair, symptom, or part... [Search]   │
│ [Cooling] [Heating] [Electrical] [Motors]     │
│ Popular repairs: Capacitor $275, Fan $650     │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

2. Convert The List Into A Service Menu

Keep the existing six categories, but add a sticky category rail on desktop and scrollable chips on mobile. It should feel like a repair menu, not a document.

┌────────────┬───────────────────────────────────┐
│ Categories │ Cooling + Refrigerant            │
│ Cooling    │ Compressor Replacement    Book   │
│ Heating    │ TXV Replacement           $1,200 │
└────────────┴───────────────────────────────────┘

3. Add A Sticky Quote Panel

Put a desktop side panel beside the list: "Not sure what failed?", same-day response, phone/text/quote buttons, and a short note about final diagnosis.

┌─────────────────────────────┬───────────────────┐
│ Repair price list           │ Not sure?          │
│ Electrical, motors, cooling │ Call or text AR Air│
│                             │ [Call] [Text]      │
└─────────────────────────────┴───────────────────┘

4. Add "How Pricing Works" And FAQ

After the list, add short answers about diagnostic fees, emergency service, why some jobs need an appointment, and what affects the final price.

┌────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ How pricing works                              │
│ ✓ Written estimate before work                 │
│ ✓ Final price depends on diagnosis and access  │
│ FAQ: Is emergency service extra? Why book?     │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

5. Use One Small Equipment Visual

Add a subtle condenser or furnace visual near the finder, but keep the price list mostly white and utility-focused. The equipment should support the page, not decorate every row.

┌─────────────────────────────┬───────────────────┐
│ Clear repair pricing         │ small HVAC render │
│ Search + chips               │ 24/7 service      │
└─────────────────────────────┴───────────────────┘

What’s Working

  • The repair prices are transparent, which is the strongest trust signal on the page.
  • The AR Air blue/white/ink palette matches the current website.
  • The categories are meaningful and should be kept.
  • The phone, text, and quote actions are already present.

Research Notes

  • Figma recommends transparent pricing, strong CTAs, mobile optimization, and FAQ content on pricing pages.
  • Baymard product-list research supports filtering and sorting tools for long browsable lists.
  • Baymard filter guidance supports category-specific filters, visible applied filters, and mobile-friendly chips.
